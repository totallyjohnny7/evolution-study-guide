Lec 12 Life History Evolution (Ch 12)

Why organisms trade quantity for quality - and why we age.

r/K Selection & Life History Strategies

Core Idea
Life history = how organisms allocate finite energy to growth, maintenance, and reproduction. Energy devoted to one function cannot go to another - this constraint drives all trade-offs.
r-Selection (Fast Pace)K-Selection (Slow Pace)
Age at maturityEarlyLate
OffspringMany, smallFew, large
LifespanShortLong
ExamplesMayfly, mustardElephant, bristlecone pine
Trap

Selection favors the OPTIMAL BALANCE for each environment. Different environments select different trade-offs - not always more offspring OR always better quality.

Extrinsic Mortality & Life-History Predictions

Extrinsic Mortality
Extrinsic mortality = death from external causes (predation, disease, parasites). High = low probability of surviving to old age = selection favors reproducing early and fast.
High Extrinsic Mortality Predicts
  • Rapid growth / early sexual maturation
  • Large number of offspring per litter
  • Shortened lifespan
  • Opposite predictions for low-mortality populations
Guppy Experiment - Trinidad (Key Exam Case)
High-predation: rapid growth, early maturity, 2x offspring (60% smaller). After 11-year transplant to low-predation: evolved later maturity, fewer but LARGER offspring. Confirms life-history theory via real-time evolution.
Opossum Island Study (Austad)
Sepelo Island (no predators, 5,000 yrs): tendon collagen had fewer cross-links = aging more slowly. Mainland (many predators): aged faster. High extrinsic mortality drives faster senescence.
Key Rule

Selection acts MORE strongly on mutations expressed early in life. Probability of being alive is highest when young, so late-life mutations accumulate unopposed.

Why We Age - Two Hypotheses

HypothesisMechanism
Mutation AccumulationLate-life deleterious mutations accumulate because selection is too weak to purge them. Early mutations ARE purged because selection is strongest when young.
Antagonistic PleiotropyOne allele = beneficial early-life + detrimental late-life effect. Selected FOR because early benefit outweighs late cost (same gene boosts reproduction at 25, causes disease at 65).
Trap

Both hypotheses predict faster senescence under higher extrinsic mortality. Mutation accumulation = passive accumulation. Antagonistic pleiotropy = ACTIVE selection FOR the allele despite late costs.

Parental Investment & Sex Allocation

Three Sex-Allocation Rules
  • OSR bias (Robert et al.): When one sex is rarer, overproduce that sex to maximize fitness (viviparous lizard example)
  • Trivers-Willard (1973): Good-condition mothers produce sons (higher reproductive variance); poor-condition produce daughters
  • Seychelles Warblers (Komdeur): High-quality territory: daughters (helpers stay). Low-quality: sons (disperse, reduce competition)
Practice
Guppies transplanted from high- to low-predation pools evolved after 11 years to have:
Low predation = low extrinsic mortality = K-type favored: later maturation, fewer but larger offspring. The experiment confirmed life-history theory in real time.

Lec 13 Evolution of Social Behavior (Ch 16)

ESS, selfishness, and the math behind why altruism exists.

ESS & Game Theory

ESS
Evolutionarily Stable Strategy: a behavioral strategy that, if adopted by most, cannot be invaded by any alternative via natural selection alone. Once fixed, mutant alternatives cannot replace it.
Side-Blotched Lizards
Three male strategies (Orange = aggressive/large territory; Blue = guard; Yellow = sneaky) maintained by negative frequency-dependent selection (Rock-Paper-Scissors). NONE is an ESS.

Individual vs Group Selection

Individual Selection Wins
Selfish mutants in a cooperative group are favored by individual selection and take over. Individual selection = dominant mechanism. Group selection fails in practice.
Trap

Group living evolves because it benefits INDIVIDUALS (vigilance, dilution, foraging). NEVER say "for the good of the species" - that is group selection logic which individual selection undermines.

Kin Selection & Hamilton's Rule

Altruism Problem
Altruism = behavior benefiting another at a COST to the actor's own fitness. Explained by kin selection: helping relatives = passing on your alleles indirectly.
Three Fitness Types
  • Direct: own reproductive success
  • Indirect: relatives' reproductive success (same alleles)
  • Inclusive fitness: direct + indirect combined
r Values
Full siblings / parent-offspring: r = 0.5 | Half-sibs / grandchildren: r = 0.25 | First cousins: r = 0.125
Hamilton's Rule

r × B > C - altruism spreads when relatedness × benefit to recipient exceeds cost to donor. Haldane: "I would risk my life for two brothers or eight cousins."

Trap

rB > C is about INCLUSIVE fitness. Altruism CAN evolve even when personally costly, as long as rB > C. High r makes altruism much easier to evolve.

Practice
Hamilton's Rule states altruism evolves when:
rB > C: relatedness x benefit to recipient exceeds cost to donor. Low r (cousins = 0.125) requires very high B to justify cost. High r (siblings = 0.5) makes altruism much easier to evolve.

Lec 14 History of Life (Ch 3)

4.5 billion years from formation to K-T boundary.

Radiometric Dating

Principle
Radioactive parent decays to daughter at a constant half-life unaffected by temperature, pressure, or chemistry. Measure parent:daughter ratio to calculate when rock solidified.
Parent-DaughterHalf-LifeBest For
Carbon-14 - Nitrogen-145,730 yrsRecent organic material (<50,000 yrs)
Potassium - Argon1.3 billion yrsMid-ancient rocks
Uranium - Lead4.5 billion yrsEarly Earth rocks
Rubidium - Strontium47 billion yrsOldest rocks
Trap

C-14 is ONLY useful for <50,000-year-old organic material. It fully decays before deep-time rocks can be measured. For millions/billions of years, use K-Ar or U-Pb.

Origin of Life

Miller-Urey Experiment (1953) - KEY EXAM FACT
Miller and Urey simulated early Earth (no O2, electrical sparks for lightning, inorganic gases). Result: 20+ amino acids including glycine, alanine, valine. Proved organic building blocks form abiotically. Does NOT demonstrate how life itself arose.
Path from Chemistry to Life
  • Prebiotic soup: pools of organic molecules in early oceans/lakes before life
  • Murchison meteorite (1989): contained amino acids, purines, pyrimidines - possible extraterrestrial organic input
  • RNA World: RNA both stores genetic info AND catalyzes reactions (ribozymes). Could be first self-replicating molecule.

K-T Boundary Evidence

Global iridium layer (rare on Earth, rich in asteroids) + Chicxulub crater (~10 mi diameter). Energy = 100 trillion tons TNT. Killed dinosaurs + 2/3 of species. Opened niches for mammal radiation.

Practice
The Miller-Urey experiment demonstrated that:
Miller and Urey: simulated early Earth (no O2, lightning, inorganic gases) = 20+ amino acids. This proves organic building blocks arise without life. It does NOT show how life itself originated.

Lec 15 Phylogenetics & Tree of Life (Ch 4)

Reading trees, synapomorphies, and tracking trait evolution.

Phylogenetic Tree Anatomy

Six Key Components
  • Tips: terminal taxa (species, populations, or genes)
  • Branches: lineages evolving between divergence events
  • Nodes: where a lineage splits = a speciation event
  • Root: oldest ancestor from which all descend
  • Clade: an organism + ALL of its descendants
  • Sister taxa: lineages that diverged from the same immediate node
Trap

Current organisms are NOT ancestors of other current organisms. Rotating branches at a node does NOT change evolutionary relationships (only topology matters, not layout).

Monophyletic, Paraphyletic & Polyphyletic Groups

GroupDefinitionValid?Example
MonophyleticAncestor + ALL descendantsYes (= clade)Birds; Mammals
ParaphyleticAncestor + SOME (not all) descendantsNo"Reptiles" without birds
PolyphyleticMultiple unrelated ancestorsNo"Warm-blooded" animals
Synapomorphy
A shared DERIVED character inherited by all descendants of a common ancestor. ONLY synapomorphies define clades in cladistics. Ancestral/plesiomorphic characters (like "has cells") are not informative for building clades.

Parsimony & Homoplasy

Principle of Parsimony
Among alternative phylogenies, choose the one requiring the FEWEST total evolutionary changes. The most parsimonious tree is preferred, but not guaranteed to be correct if convergence is common.
Two Types of Homoplasy
  • Convergent evolution: Independent origin of similar traits in unrelated lineages (usually same environmental pressure). Camera eyes: vertebrates vs octopus. Body shape: dolphins vs sharks.
  • Evolutionary reversal: Derived character reverts to ancestral state. Cetaceans returning to aquatic life. Limblessness in snakes, glass lizards, Lerista skinks - independently evolved.

Exaptation & Transitional Fossils

Exaptation
Trait that originates for one function, later co-opted for another. Feathers: evolved for insulation/display/species recognition BEFORE being co-opted for flight. Swim bladders: lungs co-opted into buoyancy organs in fish.
Tiktaalik - Phylogeny as Prediction
Phylogeny predicted a fish-tetrapod transitional fossil in mid-Devonian (~375 mya) coastal wetland rocks. Daeschler and Shubin searched Ellesmere Island, Canada, and found Tiktaalik: weight-bearing elbows, bending wrist, neck. Proves phylogenies generate testable predictions.
Feathers Before Flight
Theropod dinosaurs had feathers long before birds. Early arms too short for flight. Functions: insulation, display, species recognition, egg incubation. Flight stroke may have evolved to capture prey. Feathers = exaptation for flight.
Practice
'Reptiles' (excluding birds) is best described as a _____ group.
Paraphyletic: includes a common ancestor but excludes birds, which evolved from within the dinosaur branch of reptiles. A monophyletic 'Reptilia' would have to include birds.

Lec 16 Species Concepts & Reproductive Isolation (Ch 13)

What is a species? How do new ones form?

Four Species Concepts

ConceptKey CriterionWeakness
Biological (BSC)Reproductive isolation / gene flowFails for fossils, asexuals, hybridizing species
MorphospeciesMorphological differences (VP = VG + VE)Environment affects morphology; arbitrary cutoffs
PhylogeneticSmallest monophyletic group with synapomorphiesSplits too finely; phylogenies unavailable for most groups
General LineageShared gene pool (metapopulation)Hard to measure gene flow thresholds

Reproductive Isolating Barriers

Three Steps of Speciation
  • Step 1: Creation of a barrier to gene flow
  • Step 2: Genetic and ecological divergence
  • Step 3: Reproductive isolation becomes intrinsic
Barrier TypeTimingExamples
Pre-mating (Prezygotic)Before mating attemptHabitat isolation (hawthorn vs blueberry flies); Temporal isolation (different flowering times in hummingbird-pollinated plants)
Post-mating, Pre-fertilizationAfter mating, before fertilizationGametic incompatibility (sperm proteins fail to bind egg); Wrong courtship dance reduces fertilization
PostzygoticAfter fertilizationHybrid inviability; Hybrid sterility (mules)
Geographic Isolation
Dispersal: organisms move to new area, splitting range. Vicariance: geological barrier (mountains, rivers, rising sea) splits existing population.
Trap - Mule Sterility

Mule sterility is POSTZYGOTIC isolation. Horse and donkey CAN mate but offspring are sterile = genes cannot flow = distinct species, despite being able to interbreed.

Allopatric vs Sympatric Speciation

Allopatric
New species evolves in geographically SEPARATE (non-overlapping) areas. Most common and best-documented mode. Geographic barrier leads to divergence then reproductive isolation. Reinforcement can increase isolation after secondary contact.
Sympatric
New species evolves WITHIN the geographic range of its ancestor. Rhagoletis pomonella (apple maggot fly): originally only on hawthorn; shifted to apple trees ~140 years ago. Benefits: reduced competition, avoided parasitoid wasps. Different blossoming times = temporal isolation in progress. Ecological speciation.
Hawaiian Crickets (Laupala)
37 endemic species; volcanic islands form sequentially (hotspot moves west). Phylogeny of crickets mirrors island formation order. Confirms allopatric speciation by sequential dispersal + colonization.
Practice
The Rhagoletis pomonella case is an example of:
Sympatric: fly shifted host plants while remaining in the same geographic area. Temporal isolation (different blossoming times for hawthorn vs apple) is developing within one region - the definition of sympatric speciation.

Lec 17 Biogeography, Speciation & Extinction (Ch 14)

Diversity through time: origination, extinction, and radiation.

Dispersal vs Vicariance

Biogeography
Study of the spatial and temporal distribution of biodiversity. Initiated by Darwin and Wallace. Integrates biology, geology, geography, and paleontology.
ProcessDefinitionExample
DispersalOrganisms move to a new geographic locationMarsupials spreading from N. America to S. America to Australia
VicarianceGeological barrier splits existing populationContinental drift; rising sea level separating island populations
Marsupials Case Study
Oldest fossil: China (150 mya). Dispersed to N. America (120 mya) then S. America then across Antarctica to Australia (55 mya). Australian marsupials are monophyletic, nested within S. American marsupials. Isolated >40 my from placental mammals = convergent evolution filling the same niches (marsupial wolf, mole, anteater analogs).
Island Biogeography (MacArthur & Wilson)

Species # = dynamic equilibrium between immigration and extinction. Larger and closer islands = more species. Habitat fragments behave like "islands" - critical conservation insight.

Standing Diversity Math

Formula
D2 = D1 + originations - extinctions. Immigration/emigration matter locally but not at global scale. α = origination rate; Ω = extinction rate.
Worked Example from Slides
  • Start: 24 species
  • Stage A: 10 originate, 6 extinct → 24 + 10 - 6 = 28 species
  • Stage B: 8 originate, 12 extinct → 28 + 8 - 12 = 24 species
  • Total Ω = 9/6my = 1.5/my; total α = 8/6my = 1.33/my
Trap

When α >> Ω for long periods = adaptive radiation. When Ω >> α too long = clade extinction. Usually they track each other closely (high "turnover" but stable diversity).

Adaptive Radiation

Definition
Adaptive radiation: α greatly exceeds Ω = rapid speciation into diverse ecological niches. Occurs in absence of competitors.
Three Triggers
  • New habitat: Hawaiian honeycreepers (>50 spp from 1 ancestor ~5 mya). Cichlids in African Great Lakes.
  • Extinction removes competitor: Cenozoic mammal radiation after K-T eliminated dinosaurs.
  • Key innovation: Elutherodactylus frogs (direct-development eggs on land = 400+ spp). Angiosperms (flowers + insect coevolution = massive diversification in Cretaceous).

Mass Extinctions - The Big Five

EventSpecies LossCause
End-Ordovician86%Glaciation, atmospheric/ocean chemistry changes
Late Devonian75%Global cooling/warming, oceanic anoxia, CO2 fall
End-Permian (Great Dying)96%Siberian volcanism, CO2 rise, ocean acidification, marine anoxia
End-Triassic80%Elevated CO2, disruption of marine calcification
End-Cretaceous (K-T)76%Chicxulub asteroid, elevated CO2, global warming
Sixth Extinction?

Cause: Humans. At current rate = 75% species loss within centuries. Passenger Pigeon (hunted extinct 1900s), Dodo (sailors), Gastric Brooding Frog (extinct 1980s), Moa (Maori overhunting).

Practice
A clade starts with 40 species. 20 originate and 8 go extinct over 10 million years. Standing diversity =
D2 = D1 + originations - extinctions = 40 + 20 - 8 = 52 species. Simple formula but watch out: you add originations and subtract extinctions FROM the starting total.
