Key Terms: Synapomorphies — Shared derived traits that define those groups 1) Phylogenetic relationships are reconstructed using shared derived characteristics (synapomorphies) 2) A monophyletic group includes all the descendents from a common ancestor. 3) Homoplasy is a shared character state, but NOT due to common ancestry (convergence & reversal). 4) Phylogenies are hypotheses that can be further tested. 5) Phylogenies can help us track evolutionary changes in traits. Species Concepts & Reproductive Isolation What is a species? ~25 different definitions Differ somewhat in their assumptions, but generally agree that: 1. Species consist of groups of interbreeding individuals that share a common ancestry 2. Species are evolutionarily independent units – different species follow different trajectories through time Phylogenetic species concept • The smallest possible groups whose members are descended from a common ancestor (monophyletic) • Possess defining or derived characteristics (synapomorphies) that distinguish them from other groups
Page 26
Some problems • Traits that are used don’t have to be ecological or physiologically significant • Often divides organisms up into fine-grained species categories • Established phylogenies are not available for most groups Morphospecies concept • Species defined on basis of measurable morphological differences • Difficult to apply in some groups (Bacteria, archaea, fungi) • Used mostly with fossils, but paleontologists need to be careful (no color, soft tissue or behavior present) • Some problems • Differences used to classify species can be defined arbitrarily and can very among scientists

VP = VG + VE Biological species concept • Groups of interbreeding natural populations • Reproductively isolated from other such groups • Useful because definition focuses on the speciation process • Other concepts help recognize species, but this definition focuses on how populations are kept separate Some problems • Reproductive isolation can't be tested on fossil species • This concept doesn't apply to asexual species • This concept has difficulties with hybridizing species General lineage species concept Species are metapopulations - Group of spatially separated populations that interact at some level (genetic exchange) Various populations exchange alleles frequently enough to be treated as the same evolutionary lineage

Individuals within the metapopulation all share the same gene pool, which remains fairly consistent over time Metapopulations Gene flow: transfer of alleles from one population to another No single definition fits all taxa Biological species concept: Looks at gene flow Morphospecies concept: Looks at morphological differences Phylogenetic species concept: Looks at synapomorphies that define monophyletic groups General lineage species concept: Looks at allele exchange frequency to define the same evolutionary lineages In many cases, all concepts will agree on species boundaries
